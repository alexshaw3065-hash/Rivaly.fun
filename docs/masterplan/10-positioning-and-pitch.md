## 1. What Rivaly is

**One line**
> Rivaly is social prediction for sport.

**The normal view (anyone who asks)**
> Rivaly is where you predict sport with people, not a bookie. Create a prediction, and others pick a side: some back you, some take you on. You watch the match together, the money's held until the result is decided, and everyone on the winning side is paid automatically.

**To a friend**
> "Make a prediction on the match, then people pick sides: some back you, some bet against you. We watch it together, the money's held until it's decided, and whoever called it right gets paid instantly. No bookie, no chasing anybody."

**Brief description (forms)**
- Short (~25 words): *Rivaly is social prediction for sport: create a prediction, people back it or take the other side, and everyone on the winning side is paid automatically.*
- Paragraph (~50 words): *Rivaly is social prediction for sport. Create a prediction on a match, and others pick a side: some back you, some take you on. Everyone watches together in a live room, the money's held until the result is decided, and the winning side splits the pot automatically. People, not a bookie.*
- Founder's version (with the problem up front): *Sports betting is already social: people argue about matches with friends on WhatsApp, Telegram and X, then place their bets alone with sportsbooks that profit when they lose. Rivaly is social prediction for sport. Anyone creates a prediction, others back it or take the other side, and everyone watches the match together, chatting and reacting live. When the result is in, the pot is split among the winners automatically. We never take a side.*

**Taglines:** "Social prediction for sport." (category) · "People, not the house." (belief) · "Settle it on Rivaly." (action) · "Called it? Get paid before full time." (matchday)

**Sport, not just football.** Football is first and the brand's soul; the NFL is live; the model works for any sport with an official result. Say "sport" in definitions, lead with football in marketing.

## 2. The problem

**The insight underneath everything:** predicting sport has always been social (the group-chat argument, the office pool, the "bet you" before kickoff) and the apps split it apart.

- **Sportsbooks made it you against the house.** You bet alone, on an app, against a company that sets the odds in its favour, wins when you lose, and limits people who keep winning.
- **Prediction markets made it a price on a screen.** They strip the people out on purpose: liquidity needs interchangeable strangers, and anonymity keeps the price honest. Comments and leaderboards are bolted on; you can't choose your opponent or watch together.
- **Betting with friends breaks down.** Someone has to hold the cash, someone doesn't pay, and people argue about what the bet even was or what the result means.
- **One prediction lives across four apps:** the argument on WhatsApp or X, the money on a sportsbook, the score on a stats app, then back to the chat to gloat or chase whoever owes you.

**Is it real?** Yes: betting with friends and arguing about matches are everyday behaviour. Be honest about its kind: Rivaly mostly meets a desire with no good product ("my money where my mouth is, against *that* person, right now") rather than curing an acute pain. Desire-led products live or die on speed and fun, which is why the 30-second room matters.

## 3. The solution

The argument, the money, the match and your people in one room, settled fairly.

| The problem | What Rivaly does |
|---|---|
| Nobody holds the money | Stakes are held in escrow until the result is decided |
| Arguments about terms | The prediction is written on the room |
| Arguments about the result | Official match data decides it, not someone's word |
| Chasing payment | Winners are paid automatically, in seconds, sometimes before the final whistle |
| No way to play strangers | Anyone can take the other side of a public room |
| Betting against a company | You play people; Rivaly only takes a fee on winnings |
| Crypto setup | Email sign-in; a wallet is made for you and hidden |
| Watching alone | Live chat, match moments and the stadium race in every room |
| Nobody rewarded for bringing people | The host earns 2% of the winners' profit |

**Is it a prediction market?** In how it works, yes (a pool, like the horse-racing tote). In what it's for, no: prediction markets produce a *price*; Rivaly produces a *contest*. Hence "social prediction".

## 4. Who it's for

**Positioning line:** Rivaly is for sports fans who'd rather predict with people than against a bookie, starting with the ones who already do it together.

**Pitch paragraph (founder's final):**
> Rivaly is for fans who already predict sport together. 38% of bettors bet with friends, 68 million Americans fill in brackets, and 57 million play fantasy sports, yet the actual bet still happens alone, against a bookie that wins when they lose. We're for friends who argue in group chats, the office pools and leagues that already put money on sport, and fans who are tired of both the bookie and the trading dashboard. And we're for creators: today, betting creators make money when their followers lose; on Rivaly, they make money when their followers play.

**Slide line:** Sportsbooks make you play against the house. Prediction markets make you trade against professionals. Rivaly lets you play against people you actually know.

**The four groups**
1. **Fans who bet with friends (the main user).** They argue in the group chat, then bet alone. Rivaly puts the argument, the money and the match in one place.
2. **Group chats, office pools and fantasy leagues.** Proof people already pay to predict with people they know; the catch is someone holds the cash and pools only happen for big tournaments. Rivaly makes it every match, sorted automatically.
3. **Creators and tipsters (how it spreads).** Today their betting income comes from followers' losses; on Rivaly hosts earn from the rooms they host, whoever wins. Hosting is a feature, not the heart.
4. **Fans leaving the bookies.** 35% of bettors have cut back on sportsbooks; Rivaly gives the "not the house" idea without the trading screen.

**Who it's not for:** professional traders (Polymarket, Kalshi serve them), solo jackpot hunters (sportsbooks), under-18s and anyone who shouldn't be betting.

## 5. Why we built it (founder story)

> I built Rivaly because I realised that in real life, predicting sport is social. You argue with your friends, you back your team, you celebrate or get roasted when the match ends. But sportsbooks stripped all of that away. You bet alone, on an app, against a company that wins when you lose. I wanted to build the social space that should have been there all along: a place where you predict with the people you know, watch it together, and settle it fairly.

## 6. Why now

> And the timing is right because people are looking for an alternative to the bookie. 35% of bettors say they already use sportsbooks less, and they're choosing platforms for trust, simplicity and fairness, not just odds. Fans are also more social than ever while they watch: most are on their phones during the match, talking to friends. Yet the bet itself still happens alone. And for the first time, the technology lets us hold the pot and pay winners instantly, without anyone touching crypto.

## 7. Why this, of everything (draft — founder to add one true personal detail)

> Because it's a problem I live every week. Sport is the thing I talk about most with my friends, and predicting it together is half the fun, but every tool we have pushes us apart. We argue in one app, bet alone in another, and chase each other for money in a third. I don't see Rivaly as a betting app. I see it as putting the people back into sport, and I think whoever gets that right builds something fans use every weekend for years. I'd rather spend my time building the thing I wish existed than anything else.

## 8. Technology

> Rivaly is a mobile-first web app built with Next.js and TypeScript, hosted on Vercel.
> - **Payments and escrow: Solana and USDC.** Stakes are held in USDC on Solana, winners are paid automatically, and every stake and payout has a public on-chain receipt. Live on devnet, moving to mainnet before launch.
> - **Sign-in and wallets: Dynamic.** Email or Google sign-in, with an embedded Solana wallet created behind the scenes.
> - **Backend: Supabase** (Postgres, auth, row-level security, realtime, storage) for live chat, live pots and the Arena feed.
> - **Live sports data: TxLINE and Big Balls Data** for live scores and match events that settle rooms automatically.
> - **Other integrations:** Helius (Solana access), Cloudinary (chat photos), Klipy (GIFs), TheSportsDB (team badges), IndexNow and Google Search Console.
> - **Developer tools:** GitHub with automatic Vercel deploys, Supabase migrations, TypeScript, ESLint and a test suite, Tailwind with our own design system and design-lint tool, Render for scheduled jobs.
> - **AI tools:** built with Claude Code (Anthropic's AI coding agent) across design, engineering, testing and research. The product itself has no AI features yet, deliberately.

**How Rivaly uses Solana**
> Rivaly uses Solana as invisible payment rails for every room, with USDC as the currency so every pot is in stable dollars. When a user signs up with email, an embedded Solana wallet is created for them in the background. When they join a room, their USDC stake moves to Rivaly's escrow wallet on Solana. We build the transaction and pay the network fee, so users never need SOL or a crypto wallet. Our server checks each transfer on-chain before the stake counts. The pot stays in escrow until the match result is confirmed by official sports data. Then the winners' shares are paid straight to their wallets in USDC, usually within seconds. Every stake and every payout has a public on-chain receipt. We chose Solana because fast, low-cost transfers make instant payouts to many winners practical, even for small stakes.

If asked about the escrow: *custodial for V1 (a Rivaly-held wallet), fully verifiable on-chain, with an on-chain program on the roadmap.*

**Category:** Consumer (a mainstream social app for sport, crypto invisible). Not SocialFi (no token) and not DeFi. Prediction markets is a fair second tag. One line: *"A consumer social app for sport, with prediction at its core, built on Solana with the crypto completely invisible to users."*

**Platform:** a mobile-first web app (PWA), installable to the home screen. Not a "dApp" (users never connect a wallet, escrow is custodial). *"A mobile-first consumer web app, installable to your home screen, with payments on Solana."*

## 9. Anything else judges should know

> **Rivaly is live, not a mock-up.** It's a working beta at rivaly.fun: real rooms on real matches, settled automatically from live sports data, with USDC stakes and payouts on Solana devnet.
> - **The room is a live viewing experience,** not a bet slip: chat, real match moments, and a "stadium" where the two sides fight for control as the game swings.
> - **Winners can be paid before the final whistle.** Rooms settle the moment their result is certain, after a short safety window for VAR.
> - **Fair by design.** Rivaly never takes a side, charges only on winnings (never on a stake), and shows the fee before anyone plays. Every stake and payout has a public on-chain receipt.
> - **Built for real phones and real networks.** Email sign-in, no wallet, no fees to stake, heavy optimisation for slow mobile connections.
> - **The social layer goes beyond the room.** The Arena has takes, receipts, friends' leagues and leaderboards, and anyone who hosts a room earns from it.
>
> **Next:** mainnet, local-currency deposits and withdrawals, and responsible-play tools (deposit limits, self-exclusion) before real money goes live.

## 10. Competition and moats

**Rivaly is not the first.** People-vs-people betting apps exist (Slips, Rebet, KUTT, BroThrow, BettorEdge, BetOpenly), prediction markets are adding social features (Polymarket Squads), and others call themselves social prediction markets (OG, OpinionKings, Kash). The category is real and crowded, which proves demand. Rivaly's *combination* is rarer: pools not just 1v1, a live viewing room, anyone can host and hosts earn, the Arena, invisible crypto. Never claim it's unique or uncopyable.

**Who could copy it:** sportsbooks can, but it undercuts their house margin; Polymarket/Kalshi can, but named opponents and private pots cut against their open-market model and regulator; peer-to-peer startups and new copycats can, easily.

**What actually protects Rivaly:** owning the community first, network effects (your rivals, record, leagues and receipts), local payments and a licence, brand ("settle it on Rivaly"), and speed. Keep pots per room: one big shared pot per event would kill the friends' space and walk into Polymarket's model.

**Fees:** 5% of the winners' profit (3% Rivaly, 2% host), kept deliberately over per-stake, total-pool and Polymarket-style fees ("you only pay when you win"). No deposit fee; withdrawals only ever pass on real costs; no cash-out. If hosts need more, change the split, not the basis.

## 11. Rules for every public answer

- **Never claim** "first", "trustless", "smart contract" (escrow is custodial) or "locked until full time" (rooms settle when the result is certain).
- **No country or demographics** in public answers (founder's call).
- **Don't bring Polymarket into pitch answers** unless asked.
- Say "create a prediction", not "make your call"; keep "call" for flavour ("Called it").
- Don't lead with "prediction market" to normal fans, with crypto, or with fees ("we never take a side" first).
- Say "beta on devnet" plainly; don't imply real-money users.
- Skip harm-reading stats (e.g. Gen Z moving investment money into betting).
